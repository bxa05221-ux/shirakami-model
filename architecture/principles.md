# Shirakami Architecture — Principles

## 1. Observation before answer

The architecture does not begin by demanding a correct answer. It begins by observing what is present in the Landscape.

## 2. Unresolved questions may remain unresolved

A question may be retained in the Dark Layer without being prematurely closed.

## 3. One Landscape, multiple viewpoints

Different viewpoints observe a shared Landscape. Viewpoints are not required to become independent personalities or specialist agents.

## 4. Time is part of the structure

Observations are connected as a Thread. A later observation may change the meaning of an earlier one without rewriting the earlier observation itself.

## 5. Revisit is a first-class operation

Past questions and observations may be revisited when time, context, or new evidence makes another observation useful.

## 6. Uncertainty is preserved

Unknown, ambiguous, or unresolved information should not be silently converted into certainty.

## 7. Catch belongs to the human

The architecture may prepare, connect, and illuminate. The moment of discovery, interpretation, or final judgment remains on the human side.

## 8. The model is replaceable

A model provider supplies intelligence to the Runtime. It is not the identity or authority of the architecture.

## 9. Runtime is replaceable

The protocol should remain meaningful even when its implementation or execution environment changes.

## 10. Reality is not automatically modified

Observation and organization do not themselves grant permission to change the external world.
